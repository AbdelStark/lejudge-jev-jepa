\begin{abstract}
Latent world-model planners such as LeWorldModel (\lewm) choose actions by minimising one number, the latent distance between an imagined final state and a goal image; that cost cannot express ``but not through the middle'' or ``do not touch the block yet''. We study \lejudge, a natural-language cost module with no LLM in the planning loop: linear probes turn every imagined latent into a few words from a closed vocabulary, a decision model (\jev, TypeSafe's System~One) answers one typed yes/no question per constraint and imagined step, and code folds the probabilities into the cross-entropy-method cost. We pre-registered three studies on PushT and report all of them. \emph{Judge-only:} on \VAL{jo_items} imagined and executed rollouts described by probe words, \jev\ reaches \VAL{jo_jev_imagined_pw_acc} accuracy and \VAL{jo_jev_imagined_pw_auroc} AUROC with expected calibration error \VAL{jo_jev_imagined_pw_ece}; five held-out paraphrases per constraint cost it at most two points, where a keyword checker drops from \VAL{jo_kw_pw_acc_pooled} to \VAL{jo_kw_pw_para_pooled}. \emph{Planning, random starts:} at the pre-registered weight no judge, not even an oracle checker run on the same probe words, changes the violation rate, because in most episodes the constraint and the goal image conflict by construction; the oracle also exposed a flaw in the original shortlist cost, which we fixed and logged before any \jev\ result on the study sets. \emph{Planning, relevance-filtered starts:} where a compliant path exists, the oracle and keyword judges lower episode violations by \VAL{s2_oracle_spatial_temporal_violation_rd} and \VAL{s2_keyword_spatial_temporal_violation_rd} points on one set (Holm-corrected paired tests), and \jev\ with its confidence gate disabled matches the oracle on another (\VAL{s3_jev_tau10_implicit_vslewm_rd} points, $p_{\mathrm{Holm}}=\VAL{s3_jev_tau10_implicit_vslewm_pholm}$) at unchanged success. The remaining gap is the world model's open-loop imagination error, measured directly: with a perfect judge the chosen plan is imagined to violate the spatial rule in \VAL{diag_oracle_spatial_imag} of episodes but does so in \VAL{diag_oracle_spatial_exec} when executed. All \VAL{cache_n} judge responses are cached; every figure regenerates offline.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Joint-embedding predictive architectures (JEPA) learn to predict future \emph{representations} rather than pixels \citep{lecun2022path,assran2023ijepa}. \lewm\ \citep{maes2026lewm} trains such a model end-to-end from pixels and plans with it by sampling action sequences, rolling them forward in latent space, and scoring each by the squared distance between the imagined final latent and the encoded goal image. The recipe is attractive because it is small (a 15M-parameter model plans PushT in under a second) and because the planner touches only one scalar cost. It is also its weakness: everything the user wants that is not ``end up looking like the goal'' has to be engineered into that scalar.

LeCun's architecture sketch draws a \emph{configurable cost module} next to the world model \citep{lecun2022path}. This paper asks a narrow, testable version of that idea: can a non-expert configure the cost with a sentence, and can the sentence be enforced inside a sub-second model-predictive-control loop without putting a language model in the loop? Our answer, \lejudge, separates three concerns that are usually entangled in LLM-based reward design \citep{ma2024eureka,rocamonde2024vlmrm}: \emph{describing} an imagined state (linear probes and a closed vocabulary), \emph{judging} a sentence against a description (a decision model that returns calibrated probabilities to typed questions), and \emph{deciding} (arithmetic in code). Figure~\ref{fig:arch} shows the pipeline.

We make no claim that \lejudge\ solves language-constrained planning. We claim to have measured, with pre-registered designs and an oracle in the same code path, where each part of such a system helps and where it is limited. Concretely:
\begin{enumerate}\setlength\itemsep{1pt}
  \item \textbf{A measured bridge from latents to words.} Linear probes on \lewm's 192-d latent recover the PushT state well enough that the vocabulary words match ground truth \VAL{probe_bucket_block}--\VAL{probe_bucket_edge} of the time on encoded frames and \VAL{probe_bucket_block_h5}--\VAL{probe_bucket_edge_h5} five imagined steps out (Section~\ref{sec:probes}).
  \item \textbf{A judge-only benchmark with an oracle.} Twelve constraints in three families, each with five held-out paraphrases and two near-miss negatives, scored on identical inputs by \jev, a keyword checker, a local LLM and an oracle checker. \jev\ is accurate, calibrated and paraphrase-robust, and fires on near-miss negatives \VAL{jo_jev_imagined_pw_fpr}--\VAL{jo_jev_executed_pw_fpr} of the time (Section~\ref{sec:judge}).
  \item \textbf{Three pre-registered planning studies} that separate judge error from cost-mechanism error from world-model error, including a null result we explain, a mechanism flaw we found with the oracle and fixed before any \jev\ result on the study sets, and a direct measurement of imagination-versus-execution mismatch (Sections~\ref{sec:s1}--\ref{sec:diag}).
  \item \textbf{A fully cached, offline-reproducible build}: every response, trace, decision and deviation from the pre-registration is committed (Section~\ref{sec:repro}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related work}
\paragraph{Latent world models and planning.} DINO-WM \citep{zhou2024dinowm} plans with frozen DINOv2 features and CEM; PLDM \citep{sobal2025pldm} argues for planning with latent dynamics learned from reward-free data; TD-MPC2 \citep{hansen2024tdmpc2} learns a latent model jointly with a value function. \lewm\ \citep{maes2026lewm} is the first of these trained end-to-end from pixels without a pre-trained encoder, and ships in \texttt{stable-worldmodel} \citep{swm2026}, whose solver interface (a pluggable per-candidate objective) we build on. All of these use a single goal-distance objective; constraints, when present, are hand-coded costs.
\paragraph{Constraints in control and safe RL.} Constrained MPC and safe RL encode rules as inequality constraints or penalties on state \citep{garcia2015safe}; the constraint functions are written by an engineer. We keep the penalty formulation and replace the hand-written function by a judged sentence, which is why the effect size questions in this paper are about the judge and the model, not the optimiser.
\paragraph{Language as reward.} Eureka \citep{ma2024eureka} has an LLM write reward code; VLM-RM \citep{rocamonde2024vlmrm} uses a vision-language model's similarity as reward; LLM-as-a-judge \citep{zheng2023judge} evaluates text with a generative model. All put a large generative model in or near the loop. \lejudge\ instead uses a \emph{decision} model that returns probabilities to typed questions over a closed vocabulary; the vocabulary is what makes calls small enough (about \VAL{s2_jev_all_calls} calls and \VAL{s2_jev_all_tokens} input tokens per episode with memoisation) and what allows an oracle checker to score exactly the same inputs.
\paragraph{Probing.} Linear probes are the standard tool for asking what a representation encodes \citep{alain2017probes}; \citet{maes2026lewm} report that positions and angles are linearly recoverable from \lewm\ latents. We use the probes as a component with a measured error budget rather than as an analysis.

\section{Method}
\subsection{Setting and notation}
An episode starts from an observation $o_0$ with goal image $o_g$. \lewm's encoder maps images to $z\in\mathbb{R}^{192}$; its predictor rolls a latent forward under a block of actions. Following the authors' PushT protocol, one planning step is a block of 5 environment steps, the horizon is $H=5$ blocks, and the policy executes all 5 blocks before replanning. The CEM samples $S=\VAL{cem_samples}$ action sequences per iteration for $\VAL{cem_iters}$ iterations, refitting on the 30 lowest-cost candidates \citep{deboer2005cem}. For candidate $k$ the imagined latents are $\hat z^{(k)}_{1..H}$ and the base cost is $A_k=\lVert \hat z^{(k)}_H - z_g\rVert^2$. A constraint set $\mathcal{C}$ is a list of sentences $c$ with a family (\texttt{never}, \texttt{always}, \texttt{soft}) and a weight $w_c$.

\subsection{Probes and vocabulary}\label{sec:method-probes}
A ridge-regression head maps $z$ to the agent position, block position, $(\sin\theta,\cos\theta)$ of the block angle and a contact logit; it is trained on \VAL{probe_train_frames} frames from \VAL{probe_train_episodes} expert episodes encoded with the released checkpoint, split by episode. A deterministic vocabulary (\texttt{pusht@1}) turns the seven numbers into six words per step: the block's and the agent's cell on a $3\times3$ grid, whether the block polygon is within 10\,px of a wall (\emph{left/right/top/bottom edge} or \emph{none}), one of six 60$^\circ$ angle bins (\emph{upright}, \emph{tilted left/right}, \emph{on its side left/right}, \emph{upside down}), \emph{contact} (omitted when the logit is within $\pm0.5$ of zero) and \emph{block speed} (\emph{still}, \emph{slow}, \emph{fast}; thresholds 2 and 15\,px per block, calibrated on expert data). The mapping is pure; a unit test asserts that no digit ever reaches a fact.

\subsection{Constraint library}
Twelve PushT constraints were written before any result (Appendix~\ref{app:library}): eight \texttt{never} rules (e.g.\ \emph{``Keep the T out of the centre cell.''}), three \texttt{always} rules (e.g.\ \emph{``Approach the block from below.''}) and one \texttt{soft} rule (\emph{``Be gentle: move the block slowly.''}). Each has an oracle checker over ground-truth state, five paraphrases written to preserve meaning, and two near-miss negatives that a lazy matcher would confuse (for the centre rule: \emph{``Keep the agent out of the centre cell.''}). The library is frozen and hashed (\texttt{\VAL{library_sha}}).

\subsection{The judge call}\label{sec:method-judge}
One \texttt{system\_one} call carries a JSON state with two top-level fields, \texttt{constraints} (owner text, keyed \texttt{c1..cC}) and \texttt{candidates} (code-generated facts, keyed \texttt{k1..kK}, one row per step), and a dictionary of typed questions. For the hard families the question is a Noul (yes/no with $P(\text{true})$): \emph{``At step $t$ of candidate $k$, does the block or agent violate constraint $c$ exactly as written in \texttt{constraints.c}?''}, with criteria that name the six fact fields and say what to answer when the facts do not mention what the constraint needs. For \texttt{soft} constraints the question is a five-level Score (\emph{not at all} \ldots\ \emph{fully}) per candidate. Templates are in Appendix~\ref{app:bank}; they follow the model's published guidance \citep{typesafe2026jev}: literal wording, one judgment per question, true means present, no counting or comparison, and separate fields for trusted and untrusted text. Aggregation is code: $\max_t p$ for \texttt{never}, $1-\min_t p$ for \texttt{always}, $1-\mathbb{E}[\text{level}]/4$ for \texttt{soft}.

\subsection{Cost}\label{sec:method-cost}
Per CEM iteration the objective returns, for every candidate,
\[
\mathrm{cost}_k = \frac{A_k-\bar A}{\sigma_A} + \lambda \sum_{c\in\mathcal{C}} w_c\, \mathrm{penalty}_c(k),
\]
with $A$ standardised over the population so that $\lambda$ is unitless; $\lambda=0$ is bit-identical to \lewm\ (regression test). A confidence gate sets the penalty to zero for a candidate when more than $\tau$ of its per-step probabilities fall in $[0.3,0.7]$ (or, for Score, when the model's confidence is below $\tau$).

\emph{Which candidates are judged.} The original design (RFC-0004) judged only the $K=16$ lowest-$A$ candidates on the last three CEM iterations, reasoning that $A$ already ranks. Section~\ref{sec:s1} shows, with a perfect judge, that this cannot change plans: when the goal pulls into the forbidden region every low-cost candidate violates, the CEM refits on the unjudged candidates ranked just below, and a converged sampler cannot be redirected late. We therefore judge \emph{every} candidate. Because a hard-family question depends only on one step's words and its index, the population's $S\times H=1{,}500$ steps collapse to unique $(t,\text{facts})$ keys that are judged once and memoised across iterations, replans, episodes and runs (\VAL{cache_steps} keys after all studies); candidates inherit their steps' probabilities. Soft constraints, whose question is per candidate, are judged on the $K=16$ lowest-cost candidates, the rest receiving the shortlist's mean penalty as a prior so that an unjudged candidate is never preferred merely for being unjudged. CEM iterations $0,5,\ldots,25$ and the last are judged. This change was logged before any \jev\ result on the study sets (Appendix~\ref{app:deviations}).

\subsection{Baselines}
All judges implement the same interface and receive byte-identical states and question keys. \textbf{Oracle-on-probes} runs each constraint's ground-truth checker on the probe outputs; it is the upper bound for a judge of the given words. \textbf{Keyword} is a regex dictionary written from the canonical sentences only and applied verbatim to paraphrases. \textbf{Local LLM} sends the same JSON and question keys to Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct through an OpenAI-compatible endpoint and asks for a JSON object of probabilities (a funded hosted-LLM comparison was not available at build time).

\section{Experimental setup}
\paragraph{Environment, data and protocol.} \texttt{swm/PushT-v1} \citep{swm2026,florence2022ibc,chi2023diffusion}, the released \lewm\ PushT checkpoint (revision \texttt{\VAL{ckpt_rev}}), and the authors' evaluation protocol: start states are frames of held-out expert episodes, the goal is the frame 25 environment steps later, the budget is 50 steps, success is the environment's pose criterion, and the episode ends at success. Expert states and actions come from the official dataset; the action standardiser is fitted on all readable expert actions. \textbf{Reproduction gate.} Unconstrained \lewm\ reaches \VAL{m0_success_h3} success on \VAL{m0_success_h3_n} episodes with the paper's three-frame context (\VAL{m0_success_h1} with one). With our own random-push data the same planner reached \VAL{m0_weak_success}, because the model was trained on expert motion and a CEM fed the wrong action scale exploits it; we report this because it is the kind of silent failure that makes planning papers hard to reproduce.
\paragraph{Constraint sets.} The pre-registered sets \texttt{edges} and \texttt{edges+upright} were replaced before any study run: under the expert-start protocol the block reaches a side wall in 9 of 1{,}493 windows and is upright at the start in a minority of episodes. The study sets are \textbf{spatial} $=$ \{centre\_avoid\}, \textbf{spatial+temporal} $=$ \{centre\_avoid, no\_contact\_first3\} and \textbf{implicit} $=$ \{gentle, approach\_below\}, chosen from constraints whose base violation rate under unconstrained \lewm\ lay between 30\% and 80\%.
\paragraph{Conditions and metrics.} \{\lewm, oracle-on-probes, keyword, \jev\} $\times$ 3 sets $\times$ seeds \{0,1,2\} $\times$ \VAL{s1_episodes_per_cell} episodes per cell; $\lambda=1$, $\tau=0.5$. Conditions share start states, goals and CEM sampler seeds. Primary metrics: episode violation rate (the oracle checker on executed ground-truth states at the planner's cadence) and success rate. We report 95\% percentile-bootstrap intervals (10{,}000 resamples; \citealp{efron1979bootstrap}), paired Wilcoxon signed-rank tests against \lewm\ on per-episode outcomes \citep{wilcoxon1945} with Holm correction across the three sets \citep{holm1979}, and risk differences with bootstrap intervals. Studies 2 and 3 were pre-registered after Study~1; the pre-registration file and every amendment are hashed in the decision log \citep{nosek2018prereg}.

What matters for the judge is word accuracy: block cell \VAL{probe_bucket_block}, agent cell \VAL{probe_bucket_agent}, angle bin \VAL{probe_bucket_angle}, edge \VAL{probe_bucket_edge}, contact \VAL{probe_bucket_contact}. Rolling the predictor open-loop with the expert's own actions (\VAL{probe_imagined_n} starts), block-cell accuracy falls to \VAL{probe_bucket_block_h5} and block error grows to \VAL{probe_block_err_h5_px}\,px at $h=5$, while edge stays at \VAL{probe_bucket_edge_h5} (Figure~\ref{fig:decomp}a). An MLP head improves the angle ($R^2=\VAL{mlp_r2_angle}$, bin accuracy \VAL{mlp_bucket_angle}) but not the cell (\VAL{mlp_bucket_block}); we use the linear probe throughout.

\paragraph{Paraphrases and negatives.} \jev's accuracy on paraphrases is \VAL{jo_jev_imagined_pw_para_acc} (imagined) and \VAL{jo_jev_executed_pw_para_acc} (executed) against \VAL{jo_jev_imagined_pw_acc} and \VAL{jo_jev_executed_pw_acc} on the canonical text; the keyword checker falls from \VAL{jo_kw_pw_acc_pooled} to \VAL{jo_kw_pw_para_pooled}. The price of reading meaning rather than tokens is visible on the near-miss negatives: \jev\ fires on a sentence that swaps the object or the wall (\emph{``Never let the T touch the top edge''} when the block touches the left edge) \VAL{jo_jev_imagined_pw_fpr}--\VAL{jo_jev_executed_pw_fpr} of the time, the keyword checker \VAL{jo_keyword_imagined_pw_fpr}. \paragraph{Cost.} \jev\ takes \VAL{jo_jev_imagined_pw_lat}\,s per 1{,}000 judgments (one call judges hundreds of questions); the local 7B LLM takes \VAL{llm_lat}\,s and returned schema-valid JSON in only a third of calls (on those, accuracy \VAL{llm_valid_acc}, AUROC \VAL{llm_valid_auroc}, \VAL{llm_items} items).

Table~\ref{tab:s1} and Figure~\ref{fig:forest1} show a null result: every condition, including the oracle, sits within a few points of unconstrained \lewm\ on all three sets (\VAL{s1_n_significant} of nine Holm-corrected tests significant), at unchanged success. Because the oracle judge is by construction correct about the words it is given, this cannot be a judge failure. Two facts explain it. First, on the spatial set \VAL{s1_spatial_unsat_pct} of episodes have the goal block \emph{inside} the centre cell, so success and compliance are mutually exclusive; stratifying post hoc by whether the start and goal states allow compliance, \lewm\ violates \VAL{s1_lewm_spatial_sat_viol_pct} of the \VAL{s1_spatial_sat_n} satisfiable spatial episodes and every condition produced identical plans on them (no imagined candidate entered the centre). Second, in the conflicting episodes the original shortlist mechanism was inert: the traces of the oracle run show the penalised fraction of the population rising to 100\% while the CEM converges, i.e.\ every low-cost candidate violates and the sampler has nothing compliant to refit on. This is what led to judging the whole population (Section~\ref{sec:method-cost}); Study~1 was rerun with the amended cost and the numbers above are from that rerun, but the conclusion is unchanged because the episodes themselves do not admit compliant success. The ablations (Table~\ref{tab:abl}) confirm that nothing in the cost, $\lambda\in[0.25,4]$, $K$, schedule, hard rejection, vocabulary or probe, moves these episodes: violation stays in \VAL{abl_viol_min}--\VAL{abl_viol_max} and success in \VAL{abl_succ_min}--\VAL{abl_succ_max}.

\section{Discussion}
\paragraph{What the decision model buys.} A calibrated probability per (constraint, step) from a closed vocabulary, robust to how the rule is phrased, at a cost of a few seconds per thousand judgments and without generated text that could steer the planner. The near-miss false positives are the flip side of that robustness and are the first thing to fix in the question bank: a criterion that names the object and the wall explicitly, or a second Noul that asks whether the constraint applies at all.
\paragraph{What it does not buy.} A better world model. The largest term in the error budget is the imagination-versus-execution gap of a 15M-parameter model rolled 25 environment steps open-loop, and no judge can see through it. Study~1 is also a reminder that ``the constraint conflicts with the goal'' is not a corner case in goal-image planning: a goal image specifies the whole final state, so any rule about where the object may go is either irrelevant or in direct competition with success.
\paragraph{What we would do next.} Constraint-aware goal selection (retrieve a goal that satisfies the rule), shorter open-loop horizons with a model trained for them, and a calibrated gate (the current one is a heuristic on the band $[0.3,0.7]$; Study~3 suggests it is at best neutral and possibly harmful).

\section{Limitations}
PushT only, one world model, twelve constraints, and 90 episodes per cell, which resolves differences of about ten points and not less. The relevance filter of Study~2 uses the expert trajectory to define tension, which selects for episodes where the demonstrated route is the violating one; a different notion of tension could select differently. The oracle-on-probes upper bound is an upper bound for a judge of \emph{these words}; a richer vocabulary might move it. \jev\ is a closed model, pinned to the version that answered (\texttt{jev-1.13.0}) and fully cached, but not reproducible against a future version. The local LLM baseline is a 7B model on a small subset that mostly failed the output schema; it bounds a class of baselines from below rather than characterising it.
